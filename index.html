The failure modes are different too: a home drain clogs gradually, but a retail main can go from fine to flooding in a single busy Saturday.

Commercial Kitchens: Grease on a Schedule

Factoria's restaurants — from quick-service spots to full kitchens — live and die by their grease management. Here's what many operators learn the hard way: the grease trap is only half the system. The lines downstream of the trap still accumulate FOG over time, especially the long horizontal runs to the building main. A trap that's pumped on schedule but connected to a line that's never been jetted will still back up, usually during the busiest service of the week.

Our commercial kitchen program covers both: trap pumping on a calendar matched to the menu's grease output, plus periodic jetting of the downstream lines to keep them at full diameter. We also handle the compliance side — disposal manifests and service records that health inspectors ask for. If your kitchen has needed emergency drain service more than once in the past year, the system is telling you it wants maintenance, not another rescue.

Catch Basins and Parking Lot Flooding

Factoria's big parking lots are catch-basin country — dozens of grated inlets feeding underground storm lines, all of them collecting sediment, trash, and automotive fluids all summer. Come the first heavy fall rain, an unmaintained system does exactly what you'd expect: water ponds around the basins, spreads across driving lanes, and in bad cases finds its way into ground-floor retail spaces.

The maintenance is straightforward and best done in late summer: vacuum every basin, jet the connecting storm lines, verify outfalls are clear, and document it all. We run dedicated storm-drain routes through Factoria every August and September for exactly this reason. Property managers who've lived through one flooded Black Friday parking lot never skip the schedule again.

What to Watch For

Commercial operators should watch for the early warnings: floor drains that gurgle, restrooms that drain a beat slower than they used to, odors near kitchen or mop-sink drains, and any history of backups — even "minor" ones that cleared on their own. Each is a sign that lines are narrowing. Caught early, it's a scheduled jetting. Caught late, it's an emergency call during business hours.
